The mathematical proof of the probabilistic equation in Naive Bayes classification is based on Bayes' theorem, which can be expressed as P(Y|X) = (P(X|Y)P(Y)/P(X)). This equation represents the posterior probability of the target variable Y given the predictor variables X. The Naive Bayes algorithm assumes that the predictor variables X_1, X_2, ..., X_n are independent of each other, given the target variable Y, which enables the factorization of the likelihood as P(X|Y) = P(X_1, X_2, ..., X_n|Y) = prod_i=1^n P(X_i|Y).

The factorization of the likelihood is a key step in the mathematical derivation of Naive Bayes classification. By assuming independence among the predictor variables, the algorithm can simplify the calculation of the posterior probability and make predictions based on the likelihood of each class given the predictor variables. The use of a Gaussian distribution to model the likelihood of each predictor variable is a common approach in Naive Bayes classification, which allows for the calculation of the posterior probability using Bayes' theorem.

The mathematical derivation of Naive Bayes classification involves two main steps: the derivation of Bayes' theorem and the factorization of the likelihood. The derivation of Bayes' theorem provides the foundation for the algorithm, while the factorization of the likelihood enables the simplification of the calculation of the posterior probability. The resulting equation, P(Y|X) = (P(X|Y)P(Y)/P(X)), represents the posterior probability of the target variable given the predictor variables, which is the core of the Naive Bayes classification algorithm.
